How Modern French Drains Capture & Redirect Heavy Runoff

A properly engineered French drain relies on gravity to gather standing water through perforated subsurface piping surrounded by clean washed aggregate and non-woven filter fabric. Water quickly enters the gravel trench and flows smoothly toward designated municipal storm channels or dry wells.

  • Clean washed gravel bedding providing rapid lateral water percolation into pipe perforations

  • Pop-up emitter or daylight discharge outlets releasing captured stormwater at legal discharge boundaries

Catch Basins & Gutter Downspout Integration

Connecting roof downspouts directly into closed subsurface drainage lines prevents thousands of gallons of roof runoff from dumping near residential foundations. Catch basins placed in low-lying turf valleys capture sudden pooling before water can enter garages or living areas.

  • Direct underground downspout tie-ins redirecting concentrated roof water away from slab perimeters
  • Inline debris filter baskets preventing leaves and roof shingle grit from obstructing underground lines
  • Heavy-duty traffic grates capable of supporting lawnmowers and pedestrian foot traffic
  • Laser slope verification ensuring positive downward gradient toward discharge points

Frequently asked questions

How deep should a residential French drain trench be excavated?

Typical residential French drain trenches are excavated 18 to 24 inches deep and 10 to 12 inches wide, allowing ample space for a 4-inch pipe encased in clean washed stone and wrapped in filter fabric.

Can a French drain resolve standing water on paved outdoor areas?

Yes. Combining trench drains or channel grates along hardscape perimeters with a subsurface French drain effectively captures patio runoff and carries it away from paved outdoor living spaces.
